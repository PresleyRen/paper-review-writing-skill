# Paper Draft

Turn the author's available material into the requested manuscript passage.
Keep the author's priorities and evidence status intact.

## Load only relevant context

1. Identify the requested section, language, audience, length and output format.
2. Read the supplied notes and relevant evidence. Read the project-root ACADEMIC_WRITING.md if it exists; do not search unrelated projects for it.
3. Use supplied or approved writing samples in the same genre. A personal style profile is useful only when grounded in those samples.
4. Accept fragments, digressions, reversals, mixed language and incomplete explanations. Do not require the author to produce an outline first.
5. For examples or exercises, read references/rough-note-exercises.md. Do not import its invented facts into a real paper.

## Establish a small fact map before prose

Distinguish observations, implemented behavior, planned experiments, hypotheses, derivations and unknowns. Preserve meaningful corrections such as “actually, that was measured on the smaller split.”

For each material result, retain the method, comparator, evaluation unit, denominator, condition, budget, magnitude and uncertainty supplied by the author. Treat supplied facts as supplied facts; independent verification requires the underlying material.

A plan does not become an experiment. A working implementation does not become an empirical improvement. A plausible explanation does not become an identified cause.

Use an internal working map for a short passage. Use the project's claim ledger only when a project-scale task needs durable tracking. Do not put classification labels or working notes into manuscript text.

If the notes conflict on a material number or setting, inspect the available records. If that does not resolve it, write the supported part and give one specific, separate question or blocker. Do not guess.

## Choose information order from the material

Find what the author is trying to get across first: an observation, result, design choice, unresolved problem, or argument. Use that emphasis where it works for the section.

Make reference and causal relationships intelligible without forcing every paragraph through background–gap–method–result–significance. Do not fix the number of background sentences. Follow an actual venue's required abstract structure when one is supplied.

Preserve a genuine discovery sequence when it explains a decision. Do not invent surprise, failed attempts, lab experience or motivation to simulate a person.

Connect sentences through their actual subjects and evidence. Use familiar technical words, accurate verbs and consistent terminology. Preserve a clear ordinary sentence even if it is predictable.

## Write and check

- Draft the requested passage using the available evidence and appropriate scope.
- Keep useful author wording, emphasis, local qualifications and coherent irregular rhythms.
- Place a necessary qualification beside the claim it qualifies, including in an abstract.
- Do not scatter reviewer dialogue, drafting explanations, apologetic preambles or generic disclaimers through the manuscript.
- Check that numbers, signs, comparisons, negations, technical terms and evidence levels have not changed.
- Check that every new factual or literature claim has supplied or retrieved support. Do not add references from memory to make the passage look scholarly.
- Do not optimize for a claimed detector threshold, syntactic quota or watermark outcome.

## Deliver according to the requested genre

For manuscript-only requests, return only the supported manuscript text. If a material gap prevents faithful completion, keep its minimal note separate.

For an exercise, show the rough input, one appropriate revision and the few consequential decisions. Do not add a lengthy editorial checklist.

For a requested research plan, keep planned actions in the future. The plan may describe predictions and discriminating tests; it may not manufacture results.

When working on files, preserve the raw notes unless the user requests an in-place transformation. Save prose in the manuscript and material unresolved issues in the existing review record. A routine passage needs no new report.
